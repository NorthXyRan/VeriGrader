Evaluation

User Study

12 teaching assistants with grading experience graded real student responses to two OSQs, one on data structures and one on numerical methods, both by hand and with VeriGrader. We asked whether VeriGrader makes grading faster and more accurate, how well the LLM grades on its own, whether instructor exemplars help, and whether graders agree more.

Faster and more accurate than grading by hand

VeriGrader cut grading time by 56.3%, from 3.82 to 1.67 minutes per response, and raised accuracy (F1) from 86.26% to 91.86%. Every participant graded faster, and 10 of 12 graded more accurately.

Strong on its own, stronger with your exemplars

With no exemplars and no human input, the LLM already reached 89.4% average accuracy. Instructor-verified exemplars raised it to 92.1% and improved how closely its pieces match the ground truth in 7 of 8 settings. After the instructor’s final review, accuracy averaged 95%.

Graders agree more

Agreement on total scores (weighted Fleiss’ κ) rose from grading by hand to grading with VeriGrader: 0.485 → 0.771 on OSQ1 and 0.812 → 0.916 on OSQ2. Per scoring point (Gwet’s AC1), VeriGrader lifted OSQ1 from 0.752 with the LLM alone to 0.877, while OSQ2 stayed high (0.827 and 0.801).

Easier to use, and you keep the final say

Participants rated VeriGrader far above the manual tool for ease of use (6.42 vs. 4.33) and for how well its functions fit together (6.50 vs. 3.58). They valued seeing which piece earned each mark and why, keeping control over final scores, and spending less time grading.
