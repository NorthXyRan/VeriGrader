How it works

Review a few, scale to the rest

The LLM drafts every mark, one scoring point at a time. You verify the papers that need it. Everything you confirm becomes an example that steers how it grades the rest.

  1. Upload The question, a reference answer with scoring points, and the student responses
  2. Grade · verify · steer The loop below, repeated until every paper is confirmed
  3. Export Every score with its pieces, scoring points, labels and reasons

Stage 1 · Grading with the LLM

It grades the way you would

  • A chain-of-thought prompt has the model split each response by scoring point, label every piece Correct Wrong or Unclear, and say why.
  • Every call returns ⟨piece, scoring point, label, reason⟩ in a fixed JSON schema; the score is credited from those quadruples.
  • Unclear is a real option, so borderline pieces come to you instead of being forced into a verdict.

Stage 2 · Instructor verification

Every call, in plain sight

  • Response, reference answer and rationale sit side by side. Point at a piece and its scoring point and reason light up.
  • Relabel, remap, remove or add pieces; the score updates as you go.
  • Priority dots say where to look first: orange when 25–50% of pieces are Unclear, red above 50% or when pieces overlap.

Stage 3 · Feedback steers the model

Your judgement, applied everywhere

  • Fix what needs fixing and confirm. Save a corrected piece as a point-level exemplar, or set a whole paper as Standard to make it a response-level exemplar.
  • Exemplars join the prompt as few-shot examples. Regrade one paper, only the flagged ones, or every unconfirmed paper. No prompt writing.

+2.7% average accuracy once participants’ exemplars were added

≈ +5.6% after the final human review

Both relative to the first LLM pass without exemplars. User study, 12 teaching assistants.

Built on four design principles

  • R1 Fine-grained, interpretable feedback Every mark traces back to a piece, a scoring point and a reason.
  • R2 Learnable, adaptable scoring Your corrections feed back, so grading adapts to your standard.
  • R3 Intuitive visual hints Verdicts are drawn on the response text itself.
  • R4 Interactions that steer Click, relabel, confirm. The prompt builds itself.
